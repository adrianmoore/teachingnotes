\subsection{Simple examples to analyse and score}
\begin{itemize}
\item Melodic writing eg. Married life video from `Up' (a waltz in the original)
\item Score a simple melody for 16 bars. Analyse and feedback
\item Other examples: Star Wars (march), Superman (march), 
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sunshine - sci-fi film} 
\begin{itemize}
\item Sunshine (Danny Boyle - 2007) clip 
\item Consider spotting some music to the opening sequence. 
\item eg. Brahms1 (perhaps useful to quickly score in Sibelius and MuseScore) 
\item Gladiator (Title 1, chapter 2 - battle scene) eg. spot with Holst Mars and compare original
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Using scoring programs}
Obviously some of these programs are available to students. There are student `discounts'. MuseScore remains free.
With MuseScore, you might find more suitable soundfonts (sf2). 
\begin{itemize}
\item Sibelius
\item Dorico
\item MuseScore
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Using sample libraries}
It is worth talking about sample libraries. It is also worth (when possible) scoring for a small number of live instruments, directing live recordings. Depending upon the genre of film, the score may be orchestral, electronic, pop, dance, or electroacoustic. It may even be a hybrid of a number of these elements. 
\begin{itemize}
\item Spitfire
\item Halion
\item BBC SO
\item NotePerformer
\item Free alternatives (including soundfonts, sforzando player)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Harmony}
It is worth knowing something about musical harmony.
\begin{itemize}
\item It may be that you start with a short score (piano) and orchestrate outwards
\item Barber adagio for strings (Platoon, Stone)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Orchestration}
It is worth knowing something about the instruments of the orchestra
\begin{itemize}
\item Examples: Woodwind (Stravinsky); Brass (Janacek, Copland): Activity: copy in scoring programme to understand some of the techniques and ranges.
\item Examples. Vertigo (1958, Hitchcock - Hermann); Scott of the Antarctic (1948, Vaughan Williams); 
\item Robin Hood 1938, Korngold score; 1991, Dir: Kevin Reynolds, Music - Michael Kamen; 2010, Dir. Ridley Scott, Music - Marc Streitenfeld  
\end{itemize}


\section{Writing something more experimental}
\begin{itemize}
\item `Alternative' uses of Cubase moving towards a more sfx / electroacoustic piece
\item The USSStoolkit may also be useful, particularly for basic granulation to generate texture (although if Cubase is available, Padshop is a handy alternative)
\item Become familiar with portable recorders to gather sfx/Foley (though there are now plenty of libraries of free sound recordings online)
\item ADR - In addition to understanding the reason for ADR, in some submissions it will be important to put some of the dialogue back using students' own voices. Although your voice will not sound like the actor's original, this will give the video clip renewed `completeness'.
\item Examples: Clockwork Orange (1971, Wendy Carlos); Bladerunner (1982, Vangelis); Social Network (2010, Trent Reznor, Atticus Ross); Forbidden Planet (1956, Barron); The Birds (1963, Hitchcock, Sala and the Trautonium)
\item Examples: Bourne sfx; Wall-e; THX1138
\end{itemize}



\section{Visual Music}

\item GEM in puredata might be useful.
\item Examples: Brett Battey; Louise Harris
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}
